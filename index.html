About

I run Raphus Solutions, an AI-enabled digital transformation company in Hyderabad, and lead HorizonTrax, its AI-first education platform. Alongside that, I build an independent research portfolio in LLM evaluation and safety — spanning hallucination theory, prompt-injection security, cognitive biases in language models, and multilingual tokenization — aimed at a funded PhD in AI/CS.

My path here is non-traditional: a B.Tech in Mechanical Engineering and an MBA in Finance/HRM, followed by hands-on AI work — RLHF data work at Scale AI and Appen, applied deployments at Raphus and HorizonTrax, and a growing set of self-directed empirical and theoretical research artifacts, each shipped as open code rather than left as slide decks.

Research Interests

I work at the intersection of applied AI and its failure modes — where models are uncertain, manipulable, or biased — and how to evaluate and mitigate that rigorously.

Projects

llm-grounding-deficit

Three Lenses, One Gap: A Unified Theoretical Account of Hallucination in LLMs

Theory paper (ACL/EMNLP-track) + companion evaluation harness

Introduces a "grounding deficit" construct Δ(M,F) = (δS, δC, δE), synthesizing statistical/PAC learning theory, computability and information theory, and epistemology of language into one account of why LLMs hallucinate. Revised across two peer-review-style rounds. The companion repo implements the paper's proxy metrics as a working harness against OpenAI, Anthropic, and Together AI backends, with a Streamlit dashboard.

prompt-injection-lab

Evaluation Framework for Indirect Prompt Injection in RAG Systems

Built on the BIPIA benchmark (Microsoft, KDD 2025)

An evaluation harness for indirect prompt injection against retrieval-augmented pipelines, run across Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and Gemma. Designed to run at zero cost on Kaggle or Colab's free GPU tier, so results are reproducible without infrastructure access.

Tokenizer-Bias

Tokenizer Bias in Hindi and Telugu Text

Llama BPE vs. UTF-8 byte-level encoding, IndicCorp v2

A research-grade empirical comparison of subword tokenization behavior on two low-resource Indic languages, with an extension connecting results to Hofmann et al.'s "Compute-Optimal Tokenization." A June 2026 pass diagnosed a dummy-tokenizer bug, removed unreliable numeric claims, and added an explicit Limitations section.

llm-cognitive-biases

Replicating Tversky & Kahneman Paradigms Against LLMs

Conjunction fallacy · framing · anchoring · base-rate neglect · availability heuristic

Tests five canonical cognitive-bias paradigms against published human baselines, using the exact original item wording. Includes a model runner supporting the free-tier Hugging Face API and local/Colab GPU backends, response scoring, Wilson confidence intervals, Cohen's h, and a full Jupyter analysis notebook.
