THE CENTRAL QUESTIONHow do we move from impressive AI demonstrations toward systems whose behaviour can actually be understood, measured and trusted?

The short version

An unconventional route into AI research — through engineering, product building and independent empirical work.

My background began in mechanical engineering, followed by formal study in computer applications and an MBA focused on finance with a minor in HR. Alongside that trajectory, I moved deeper into software, machine learning and large language models through hands-on system building.

That combination shaped the way I approach research. I am interested not only in what a model can produce, but in the mechanisms, representations, evaluation choices and deployment conditions that shape its behaviour.

Today, my work increasingly centres on empirical questions around foundation models, AI safety, LLM reliability, multilingual behaviour, cognitive patterns and efficient AI. I use public artifacts, experiments and technical writing to make those questions concrete.

“Build the system. Measure the behaviour. Make the evidence inspectable.”

Research philosophy

I prefer questions that can survive contact with an experiment.

A compelling AI claim is only the beginning. I want to know how the claim can be operationalized, what baseline it should be compared against, which assumptions the measurement introduces, and whether another researcher can reproduce the result.

This is why my portfolio puts public artifacts alongside research writing. A repository, evaluation, visualization or interactive system can expose parts of an idea that prose alone cannot.

I am especially interested in the space between broad questions and measurable behaviour: how an abstract concern such as reliability, safety, bias or sustainability becomes a concrete experimental object.

01 / QUESTION

Make the question narrow.

Turn a broad research interest into a falsifiable question with a defined object of study.

02 / EVIDENCE

Measure before interpreting.

Use controlled comparisons, published baselines and transparent evaluation wherever the question permits.

03 / ARTIFACT

Leave something inspectable.

Code, experiments, visualizations and technical notes should make the reasoning easier to examine.

04 / REFLECT

State the limits.

Results are more useful when their scope, assumptions and failure modes are visible rather than hidden.
